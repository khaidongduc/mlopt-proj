\begin{abstract}
This paper considers the problem of reducing label complexity for large datasets, as a means to reduce the cost of performing supervised learning with fewer labels. Despite the strengths of machine learning applications, training for high-dimensional problems requires greatly more labeled data to achieve optimum regression weights. As these learning algorithms become larger in dimension, more data will be needed. The human cost of labeling is great, so it is desired to reduce the need for labeling data as much as possible.
In this paper we present our chaining sampling algorithm that reduces the label complexity of a dataset. We examine two case studies to see the effectiveness of this algorithm in real and synthetic data.
\end{abstract}
